How to: Long Multiplication & Division

Two simple ways to work them out, one step at a time.

Long multiplication and long division look tricky at first, but they're really just lots of small, easy steps done in order. This page shows you exactly what to do, with real examples worked all the way through.

Grab a pencil and some paper — the maths quiz only checks your final answer, so work it out on paper first, the same way you would in school, then type in the answer.

Long multiplication

For questions like 236 × 14

The trick is to split the second number into tens and units, multiply by each part separately, and then add the two answers together. So for 34 × 21, that means working out "34 × 1" and "34 × 20" separately, then adding them.

Example 134 × 21

Step 1. Multiply 34 by the units digit of 21, which is 1. 34 × 1 = 34.

Step 2. Multiply 34 by the tens digit of 21, which is 20 (not just 2!). 34 × 20 = 680. A quick way to do this: work out 34 × 2 = 68, then add a zero on the end to make 680, because you're really multiplying by 20, not 2.

Step 3. Add the two answers together: 34 + 680 = 714.

Example 2236 × 14

Same idea, just with a bigger first number. 14 splits into 4 (units) and 10 (tens).

Step 1. 236 × 4 = 944.

Step 2. 236 × 10 = 2360 (multiplying by 10 is easy — just add a zero on the end).

Step 3. Add them: 944 + 2360 = 3304.

Check it: round the numbers to check your answer looks right. 236 is close to 240, and 240 × 14 is about 3360 — so an answer of 3304 looks about right, but an answer like 334 or 33040 would clearly be a mistake.

Long division

For questions like 621 ÷ 23

A friendly way to do long division is to keep taking away easy "chunks" of the number you're dividing by, until there's nothing left — then add up how many chunks you took away. In the quiz, division always comes out exactly, with nothing left over, so this always works perfectly.

Example 1468 ÷ 12

We want to know how many 12s fit into 468. Start with an easy chunk: 12 × 10 = 120.

We took away 12 four times (that's 3 lots of 10, plus one lot of 9): 10 + 10 + 10 + 9 = 39.

Example 2621 ÷ 23

23 is a trickier number, but the method is exactly the same — just keep chipping away with whatever chunk fits.

StepTake awayChunks so farLeft
Start—0621
123 × 10 = 23010391
223 × 10 = 23020161
323 × 5 = 1152546
423 × 2 = 46270
Nothing left, so 621 ÷ 23 = 27

Add up the chunks: 10 + 10 + 5 + 2 = 27.

Picking good chunks: ×10 is always the easiest place to start — just add a zero. Once ×10 chunks won't fit any more, try ×5 (half of ×10), then ×2, then ×1, until there's nothing left.
